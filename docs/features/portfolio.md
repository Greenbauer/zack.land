## Reach

Portfolio in the header opens a small pane of the cards' frosted glass under it, offering Web Apps and Miscellaneous in the header links' own colours (the current page in the accent, underlined, the other in the text colour); it slides down 3rem into place, starting to fade in as it moves, and closes on a choice, a click outside or Escape, and Portfolio stays underlined on both pages (`components/Header/NavDropdown.tsx`). While it is open everything under it blurs (6px, fading over 0.3s), the 3D scene and the page alike, the whole contact form included, as the scene does behind a focused form field; the header links and the site footer stay sharp, and a click on the blur only closes the pane. The pane sits sharp on top with its own frost and lenses (the photo glass's filter, on a layer just before it, `data-glass-over`), sliding and fading in with it. In the phone menu Portfolio is a button: it turns the menu to a second view listing the two, with a way back (`components/Header/MobileNav/index.tsx`; the menu slides down from above as it opens, fading in on the way, `animate-menu-in` in `tailwind.config.js`), and stays underlined on both pages; the menu's glass is tinted deeper than a card's (`data-glass-deep`, `components/Background/glass.ts`) so its square lenses show plainly, and it covers the footer's glass. `/portfolio` itself redirects to Web Apps. Web Apps and Miscellaneous list their posts from `Contents.tsx` beside them; on a wide screen, sliding chevrons (`components/PageScroller/Chevrons.tsx`, shrunk) pointing up with the word Scroll under them sit a little under the header, centred over the 3D head, and Scroll over chevrons pointing down just over the footer, centred under it, each on a small pane of the cards' frosted glass, at half its strength, reaching well past the arrow at its sides and toward the header or footer but only a little toward the head, that fades out within 40px of it rather than a card's 160px (`data-glass-near`, `NEAR_TO` in `components/Background/glassCards.ts`) in the theme's light (`head-light`) while the head or a dark sky leaves it 4.5:1 (read from the frame itself, `components/PageScroller/arrowInk.ts`), and otherwise in the cards' text colour, which the glass keeps readable (`data-glass` on each arrow), following the head across as it moves (`framedHead` in `components/Background/headPlace.ts`), fading out as the page turns and back in once the arriving card has slid past them (`SETTLE_MS` in `ScrollArrows.tsx`), and fading in whenever they appear, as when the head comes back and the down arrow leaves the footer (`@starting-style` in `styles/globals.css`); on a short window they move in only as far as the head allows (its crown and chin are fractions of the window's height in `ScrollArrows.tsx`), and under 700 pixels tall they are smaller. On a phone, or on a wider window wherever the head has stepped off the screen to leave a post's card the room (`framedHead.x` below 0), only the down arrow shows, at the full size, in the footer, centred, taking the footer's colours, and it is all the footer shows there: the "I can make almost anything" text and the social links are left out so the posts get the room, never fading at once with anything near them: as the head steps away the text and links fade out (0.4 seconds) and only then does the arrow fade in (0.4 seconds), and as it comes back, or as the last post is reached and the arrow goes (it shows only while there is a post below), the text and links wait for the scroll arrows above to fade in (0.5 seconds) and then fade in themselves (`data-footer-arrow` and `data-footer-extra` in `components/Footer/index.tsx`, the steps in `Footer/arrowSwap.ts`, tested by `arrowSwap.test.ts`; the arrow's slot is empty on every other page, where the footer keeps its text and links), and it hides while the phone menu is open; on a wide screen they sit outside the page's scroller so its fade under the header does not hide them; each glides to the post before or after, and shows only while there is a post that way (`components/PageScroller/ScrollArrows.tsx`, through `pageGlide.ts` to `SnapPager.tsx`). A post's Visit Site and View Code open in a new tab. A Web Apps post can show a small pill above its technology saying how the site was built: Hand Coded (a raised hand, in the card's underline colour: peach on a dark card, purple on a light one) for Banner Eyewear, the only one written by hand, and Vibe Coded (sparkles) for This website, both from react-icons (`coded` in `Contents.tsx`; a post without it, as the other two and Miscellaneous, shows none). Buttons take the head's colours, sampled from its rendered skin: its brightest highlight at rest, sweeping fast through peach, rose, purple and deep blue to its near-black navy on hover, flat with rounded corners (`.bg-gradient-button` in `styles/globals.css`). On a wide screen each post is one full page: it fills the space between the header's and the footer's fades, its images above its frosted-glass card (big rounded corners; see home.md, Theme), which spans the bottom of the post: wide and only as tall as its text, and, like the images above it, starting just right of the 3D head's face so the two line up (`components/useHeadClear.ts`; unless that leaves the post under 560 pixels); its glass runs flat past the window's right side when that side is near (closer than the glass's 160px soft edge reaches: `components/edgeBleed.ts`; on a window wider than the content's 1536px frame the card ends with its own rounded edge at the frame), and on a phone past both sides, so it meets the edge square and only fades out at its top and bottom (`components/EdgeCard.tsx`), its title and description beside its technology and links, in text close to the site's normal size. Under a mouse or pen, a post's images, and a video from the site itself (not a YouTube embed), bend along the pointer's trail with their colours parting at the edges (`components/MediaBend.tsx`; see home.md). The card doesn't scroll with its post: it is fixed where it rests, and only the card of the post crossing the window's middle shows, so paging down sends one card on to the right, gathering speed off the window's right edge (0.8 seconds), and slides the next in from the window's left edge to its place, slowing to a stop (1.4 seconds, starting 0.7 seconds in); paging back up runs it the other way, right to left. The card's glass stays whole all the way, and only what is on it fades: out as it leaves, and in from a quarter of a second into the slide as it arrives. A card waits wholly off the window's right side while its post is above the one in view and off its left side while below, a window's width and 20rem away, past the reach of its glass, so it never shows before it moves (`PostPlace` in `components/useHeldCard.ts`), and moves there by transitions, so a card turned back part way reverses from wherever it is. Going to another page, the post in view leaves the same way, its card on off the right and its tiles melting off to the right, before the next page arrives (`components/PageLeave.tsx`; see home.md). A card arrives this way when the page loads too (with reduced motion the card just shows or hides and its text fades), while the images move with their posts (`components/useHeldCard.ts`). A post with no images (This website), on a wide window with no room for its card beside the head, has its card at the top of the post instead, when it ends above the head's brows (4 tenths of the window's height down), and the head stays centred under it rather than moving off the window; the card is then on its dark side, light text, whatever the sky, as the head's crown is behind it (`components/useCardOnTop.ts`, tested by `useCardOnTop.test.ts`; `data-head-avoid="above"` in `EdgeCard.tsx`, read by `Background/HeadFraming.tsx` and `Background/glassTint.ts`); on a shorter window it stays at the bottom and the head moves away. The images lie behind the card, never over its text; on a wide screen the card takes at most 55% of the post's height, but never so little that its column of links (badge, technology, Visit Site, View Code) is cut by its bottom edge or its description shows no line: the rest of the text is cut instead (`components/useCardFloor.ts`). On a wide window too short for even that, or where its photos would keep less than a 64-pixel strip above it (`MIN_PHOTOS_PX`), as on a phone on its side or at 900 by 560, the card is laid out open from the start, as More opens it: its images give way, it shows all its text and links with no More, and the keys, the wheel and a finger scroll through it, while the Scroll arrows turn straight to the next or previous post (`data-laid-open`, `SnapPager.tsx`). Nothing scrolls inside a card: on any screen, text too long for it is cut after the last whole line the card has room for, with an ellipsis, so no line is cut through or faded out (`components/FitText.tsx`, tested by `FitText.test.ts`), and More shows (`components/useClipped.ts`), on a wide screen right under the text; More opens the card in place of the images, taking the whole post, and if the text is longer still, the post grows and the page scrolls through it; Less closes it and goes back to the post's top. Neither jumps: More raises the card's top to the top of the post over 0.6 seconds, growing it upward, while the images fly up out of the window, and Less brings the card's top back down while the images fly back in from above (`components/useMoreMotion.ts`, which measures the opened card only once the images are out of the layout, and the closed card only once its text is cut to its lines; with reduced motion it just opens and closes). A post left open closes again, at once, a moment (0.9 seconds) after the page has moved on from it, and if the page comes back to it sooner it closes as it arrives, so it is always closed when the page comes back, and what is in view does not move (a turn's glide looks up where it lands each frame, so a post that closes under it still lands right). Whenever the posts change height under the page (the footer's text coming and going at the last post changes every post's height, `--post-height`; a window resized or a phone turned; a post closing above the one in view), the pager puts the page back on the post it was on, as far down it as it was and no further than its end, or at its end if it was there, as paging back up onto a post taller than the page lands (`placeIn` and `keptPlace` in `paging.ts`, tested by `paging.test.ts`; `SnapPager.tsx`): Chrome snaps back onto a post by itself, but Safari on an iPhone does not, and left the page part way between posts after a trip to the last post and back, the post's photos lying over its card, which is fixed at the bottom of the window. The images are a block of tiles: each is cropped to whichever of wide (16:9), landscape (4:3), square or portrait (3:4) is nearest its picture (from the `width` and `height` in `Contents.tsx`; a video is wide, goes first and is laid out as the largest tile, in its own shape, and with no video an image marked `main` does, as Banner Eyewear's laptop photo is), or one shape taller where that fills clearly more. They are packed edge to edge, on whole pixels so no hairline shows between them, into one solid rectangle that fills as much of their space as it can, counting more for using its height, centred across and set down at the bottom of their space, against the card, so the gap above the card is the same at any size. Only the rectangle's four outer corners are rounded; corners that face another tile are square (`components/collageLayout.ts`). When a post becomes the one in view (as its card arrives; `isShown` from `components/Post.tsx`, so even a short phone's small strip of tiles counts) once every tile of the last post has gone, each tile melts in from the window's right side after its own random wait: bands of the picture run in as liquid streaks with their colours parting, as under the pointer, and pool into place (1.3 seconds), and it rests there, still; as the turn begins, before the page has moved, each melts back off to the right the same way, gathering speed (0.85 seconds); a tile melting is drawn at the place it rests, on a canvas that hides as soon as nothing is melting, so it only ever moves sideways, never up or down with the page, a turn back reverses it from wherever it has got to, a video melts as its YouTube thumbnail and an animated GIF as its first frame (`components/MediaMelt.tsx`, `components/mediaMelter.ts`, the turn announced by `announceTurn` in `components/PageScroller/pageGlide.ts`); with reduced motion, or with no real GPU, it slides in from wholly off the window's right side instead (0.9 seconds) and slides back off (0.6 seconds), which also moves with the page; playing a video makes it take the whole post, as More does for the card: it grows over the other tiles to the post's full width, 16:9 tall or the post's whole height where that is less (the player then shows the video within it), centred, all its corners rounded, over 0.6 seconds, while the post's card fades away, the other tiles blur and the scene behind blurs as behind a focused contact field (`data-focus-blur`, `components/Background/focusBlur.ts`); pausing or ending it, a press anywhere outside it, or paging away pauses it and brings them back (`components/PostCollage.tsx`, `components/Media.tsx`, the place in `components/videoFill.ts`, tested by `videoFill.test.ts`); hovering a tile leaves its size alone: only the pointer's bend above shows (`components/PostCollage.tsx`, the motion in `.collage-tile` in `styles/globals.css`; with reduced motion they only fade). One notch of the wheel, one trackpad swipe (its coast included, however unevenly it rises and slows) or one press of an arrow, Page Up or Down or space glides straight to the next or previous post in 0.3 seconds, and so does a finger's swipe on a phone (40 pixels up or down; the browser's own scroll is held off meanwhile). A turn back the other way goes at once, even a single notch of the wheel right after a burst the other way: if the next card has not set off yet the last one comes straight back, and if it has, it slides back out the way it came while the last one returns. A push the same way while the new card is still coming in (1.4 seconds), or while the same scroll keeps going (until its pushes pause for 0.3 seconds), is let go, not kept for later, so one scroll, however long or uneven, or a burst of scrolling, turns one page and cards never pile up over each other; the page's first card arriving counts as a turn for the wheel, a swipe and the keys, though a scroll arrow, which shows as it arrives, turns at once (`components/PageScroller/SnapPager.tsx`, logic in `paging.ts`, `turnGate`, `HOLD_MS`, `GESTURE_GAP_MS` and `QUICK_MS`, tested by `paging.test.ts`). A post that More has opened taller than the page is scrolled through before the page turns: the wheel moves through it and stops at its end (a trackpad's coast stops there too), the arrow keys and the scroll arrows move most of a window at a time (in the pager's own short glide, whose end is a place in that post looked up again each frame, so a post whose height changes on the way, as one does while More's motion settles, keeps the page inside it rather than leaving it between posts; checked by "down as More opens" in `e2e/moving-views.e2e.ts`), and a finger drags through it; only a fresh push at its end turns the page, and paging back up onto it lands on its end (`throughPost`, `turnIndex` and `landing` in `paging.ts`; the browser's snapping is off while the page is on it). On a wide but short screen (800 pixels tall or less) the text is set smaller so the longest post still fits. On a phone the fades are shorter, each post still fills one screen with its image tiles above its card, its text in one column, its title a step smaller than the site's other headings (20 pixels) so it keeps to one line and leaves its images room, and a post with images shows four lines of its description (two on a screen 700 pixels tall or less) and hides the rest, with its technology, behind More (when those lines already hold its whole description, it shows its technology and no More: `phoneParts` in `components/postParts.ts`, tested by `postParts.test.ts`), unless its images are held by the phone's width: then the room the whole card takes costs them nothing (every tile keeps 97% of its width), so the card shows whole, technology and all, with no More; it checks again when the post's whole box changes (its width, or its height, which follows the window and the footer), never on its padding alone, which the whole card itself changes (the post's bottom padding is its card's height), and only while the card is closed and still, so More then Less puts the post back exactly as it was (checked by `cypress/e2e/more-less.cy.ts`) (`components/usePhoneWhole.ts`, tested by `usePhoneWhole.test.ts`, laying the images out with `layOut` from `PostCollage.tsx`); its badge sits in the title's row at its right, or on its own line at the left when the title leaves no room, and More and its links share a row, More at its left and the links at its right under the end of the text, so on a short phone its images keep about half the post. Opened by More, shown whole, or with no images, the card's links (Visit Site, View Code) end it at its bottom right, each set to the right of its column (`linkClass` from `phoneParts`).

Roles: none; every page is public. Content: the `Contents.tsx` beside each page and the images under `public/`.

2. Open `/portfolio/web-apps` at 1280 by 720: a post per entry, first "This website", each with its media and description; no card scrolls inside, and Banner Eyewear's text ends on a whole line with an ellipsis and More right under it, which shows the whole text in place of the images; one scroll moves to the next post; on the first post only Scroll's down arrow shows, clicking it moves one post, and on the last post only its up arrow shows.
3. On `/portfolio/web-apps` at 1440 by 900, one notch of the mouse wheel lands squarely on the next post with no pause, and one swipe on a trackpad moves exactly one post. Three quick notches down turn one page, then another about 1.4 seconds later; a notch up right after a notch down turns straight back, the card coming back from the right; paging up the cards slide right to left; two cards never show over each other.
4. Open `/portfolio/miscellaneous` at 375 by 812: only the down scroll arrow shows, centred in the footer, which shows nothing else, and tapping it moves to the next post; each post fits on one screen and one swipe moves to the next; More on Smart Bed shows its whole description in place of its images, the page scrolls through the rest of it before the next swipe turns the page, and Less brings its images back.
5. Open `/portfolio/miscellaneous`: every post's image, GIF or video loads, with no broken image.
6. In Safari on an iPhone, on `/portfolio/miscellaneous`, open Buoyancy Sorting Robot with More, swipe on to the last post, then swipe back: the page lands on the robot's post, its card closed, its photos above its card, none over the text. With the card open, its View Code or Visit Site sits at the card's bottom right.

The tiles' crops and layout are covered by `components/collageLayout.test.ts`, their rounded corners, stillness at rest, hover and arrival by `e2e/media-grid.e2e.ts`, the melt's timing and resting place by `components/mediaMelter.test.ts`, and which header link is underlined by `components/Header/isRouteActive.test.ts`. Two end-to-end checks run on every pull request against the built site (`.github/workflows/tests.yml`): the Cypress spec `cypress/e2e/more-less.cy.ts` (the check `Phone More/Less`), and the Playwright tests `e2e/moving-views.e2e.ts` (the check `Moving views`). The second takes a video post, a photo post and the text-only post (first on its page, so it is only left downward) and, on a phone upright (390 by 844) and on its side (844 by 390) and at 1280 by 720 and 1024 by 640, leaves each closed, and open where it opens (with More, or laid open on a short window; where a card shows whole there is nothing to open and that case is skipped, as the text-only post's is on an upright phone and at 1280 by 720), down to the next post and up to the one before, and comes back; and it resizes the window while a post is open (upright to sideways and back, sideways to upright and back, 1280 by 720 to 1024 by 640 and back, and the other way). It runs in Chromium and in WebKit, with the browser's own snapping off so the site has to keep the page on its post itself, as Safari on an iPhone needs: back on the post the page is on it, its card is closed and no photo lies over its text, and an open card on a phone has its links at its bottom right. One more case keeps the browser's snapping on, with reduced motion, on a phone held sideways: paging down Web Apps with the keys, the page comes to the end of each of its first two posts (both laid open, taller than the page) before it turns to the next, rather than being pulled back to a post's top. `cypress/e2e/navigation.cy.ts` visits these pages too, but its selectors match nothing the components render, and no workflow runs it.

## Gotchas

- A post's media `key` is a path under `public/` or, for a video, a YouTube id, and `main: true` makes an image its post's largest tile when the post has no video (`types/index.ts`); a misspelled path shows a broken image with no error. An image's `width` and `height` pick its crop; left out, it is cropped square.
- `Button` is shared with [contact](contact.md).
