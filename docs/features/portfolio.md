## Reach

Portfolio in the header opens a small pane of the cards' frosted glass under it, offering Web Apps and Miscellaneous in the header links' own colours (the current page in the accent, underlined, the other muted); it slides down 3rem into place, starting to fade in as it moves, and closes on a choice, a click outside or Escape, and Portfolio stays underlined on both pages (`components/Header/NavDropdown.tsx`). While it is open everything under it blurs (6px, fading over 0.3s), the 3D scene and the page alike, the whole contact form included, as the scene does behind a focused form field; the header links and the site footer stay sharp, and a click on the blur only closes the pane. The pane sits sharp on top with its own frost and lenses (the photo glass's filter, on a layer just before it, `data-glass-over`), sliding and fading in with it. In the phone menu Portfolio is a button: it turns the menu to a second view listing the two, with a way back (`components/Header/MobileNav/index.tsx`; the menu slides down from above as it opens, fading in on the way, `animate-menu-in` in `tailwind.config.js`), and stays underlined on both pages; the menu's glass is tinted deeper than a card's (`data-glass-deep`, `components/Background/glass.ts`) so its square lenses show plainly, and it covers the footer's glass. `/portfolio` itself redirects to Web Apps. Web Apps and Miscellaneous list their posts from `Contents.tsx` beside them; on a wide screen, sliding chevrons (`components/PageScroller/Chevrons.tsx`, shrunk) pointing up with the word Scroll under them sit a little under the header, centred over the 3D head, and Scroll over chevrons pointing down just over the footer, centred under it, each on a small pane of the cards' frosted glass, at half its strength, reaching well past the arrow at its sides and toward the header or footer but only a little toward the head, that fades out within 40px of it rather than a card's 160px (`data-glass-near`, `NEAR_TO` in `components/Background/glassCards.ts`) in white while the head or a dark sky is behind it (read from the frame itself, `components/PageScroller/arrowInk.ts`), and otherwise in the cards' text colour, which the glass keeps readable (`data-glass` on each arrow), following the head across as it moves (`framedHead` in `components/Background/headPlace.ts`), and fading out as the page turns and back in once the arriving card has slid past them (`SETTLE_MS` in `ScrollArrows.tsx`); on a short window they move in only as far as the head allows (its crown and chin are fractions of the window's height in `ScrollArrows.tsx`), and under 700 pixels tall they are smaller. On a phone, or on a wider window wherever the head has stepped off the screen to leave a post's card the room (`framedHead.x` below 0), only the down arrow shows, at the full size, in the footer at the left of its "I can make almost anything" text, a little in from the window's edge, sharing that row and taking the footer's colours (`data-footer-arrow` in `components/Footer/index.tsx`, empty on every other page), and it hides while the phone menu is open; on a wide screen they sit outside the page's scroller so its fade under the header does not hide them; each glides to the post before or after, and shows only while there is a post that way (`components/PageScroller/ScrollArrows.tsx`, through `pageGlide.ts` to `SnapPager.tsx`). A post's Visit Site and View Code open in a new tab. A Web Apps post can show a small pill above its technology saying how the site was built: Hand Coded (a raised hand, in peach) for Banner Eyewear, the only one written by hand, and Vibe Coded (sparkles) for This website, both from react-icons (`coded` in `Contents.tsx`; a post without it, as the other two and Miscellaneous, shows none). Buttons take the head's colours, sampled from its rendered skin: its brightest highlight at rest, sweeping fast through peach, rose, purple and deep blue to its near-black navy on hover, flat with rounded corners (`.bg-gradient-button` in `styles/globals.css`). On a wide screen each post is one full page: it fills the space between the header's and the footer's fades, its images above its frosted-glass card (big rounded corners; see home.md, Theme), which spans the bottom of the post: wide and only as tall as its text, and, like the images above it, starting just right of the 3D head's face so the two line up (`components/useHeadClear.ts`; unless that leaves the post under 560 pixels); its glass runs flat past the window's right side when that side is near (closer than the glass's 160px soft edge reaches: `components/edgeBleed.ts`; on a window wider than the content's 1536px frame the card ends with its own rounded edge at the frame), and on a phone past both sides, so it meets the edge square and only fades out at its top and bottom (`components/EdgeCard.tsx`), its title and description beside its technology and links, in text close to the site's normal size. Under a mouse or pen, a post's images, and a video from the site itself (not a YouTube embed), bend along the pointer's trail with their colours parting at the edges (`components/MediaBend.tsx`; see home.md). The card doesn't scroll with its post: it is fixed where it rests, and only the card of the post crossing the window's middle shows, so paging down sends one card on to the right, gathering speed off the window's right edge (0.8 seconds), and slides the next in from the window's left edge to its place, slowing to a stop (1.4 seconds, starting 0.7 seconds in); paging back up runs it the other way, right to left. The card's glass stays whole all the way, and only what is on it fades: out as it leaves, and in from a quarter of a second into the slide as it arrives. A card waits wholly off the window's right side while its post is above the one in view and off its left side while below, a window's width and 20rem away, past the reach of its glass, so it never shows before it moves (`PostPlace` in `components/useHeldCard.ts`), and moves there by transitions, so a card turned back part way reverses from wherever it is. Going to another page, the post in view leaves the same way, its card on off the right and its tiles off to the right, before the next page arrives (`components/PageLeave.tsx`; see home.md). A card arrives this way when the page loads too (with reduced motion the card just shows or hides and its text fades), while the images move with their posts (`components/useHeldCard.ts`). A post with no images (This website), on a wide window with no room for its card beside the head, has its card at the top of the post instead, when it ends above the head's brows (4 tenths of the window's height down), and the head stays centred under it rather than moving off the window; the card is then on its dark side, light text, whatever the sky, as the head's crown is behind it (`components/useCardOnTop.ts`, tested by `useCardOnTop.test.ts`; `data-head-avoid="above"` in `EdgeCard.tsx`, read by `Background/HeadFraming.tsx` and `Background/glassTint.ts`); on a shorter window it stays at the bottom and the head moves away. The images lie behind the card, never over its text; on a wide screen the card takes at most 55% of the post's height. Nothing scrolls inside a card: on any screen, text too long for it is cut after the last whole line the card has room for, with an ellipsis, so no line is cut through or faded out (`components/FitText.tsx`, tested by `FitText.test.ts`), and More shows (`components/useClipped.ts`), on a wide screen right under the text; More opens the card in place of the images, taking the whole post, and if the text is longer still, the post grows and the page scrolls through it; Less closes it and goes back to the post's top. Neither jumps: More raises the card's top to the top of the post over 0.6 seconds, growing it upward, while the images fly up out of the window, and Less brings the card's top back down while the images fly back in from above (`components/useMoreMotion.ts`, which measures the opened card only once the images are out of the layout, and the closed card only once its text is cut to its lines; with reduced motion it just opens and closes). A post left open closes again, at once, a moment after the page has moved on from it, so it is closed when the page comes back, and what is in view does not move. The images are separate tiles, not a grid: each is cropped to whichever of wide (16:9), landscape (4:3), square or portrait (3:4) is nearest its picture (from the `width` and `height` in `Contents.tsx`; a video is wide, goes first and is laid out as the largest tile, in its own shape, and with no video an image marked `main` does, as Banner Eyewear's laptop photo is), or one shape taller where that fills clearly more. They are packed to fill as much of their space as they can, counting more for using its height, centred, then nudged, tilted and grown a little so they overlap each other slightly, the same way on every load (`components/collageLayout.ts`). When a post becomes the one in view (as its card arrives; `isShown` from `components/Post.tsx`, so even a short phone's small strip of tiles counts) each tile slides in from wholly off the window's right side after its own random wait, slowing gently into place (0.9 seconds), then drifts gently on its own beat; when the post leaves, each slides back off to the right, gathering speed (0.6 seconds), and hides; no bounce, spin or fade; a video's tile always sits above the others, so none covers its controls (its full-screen button included); playing it makes it take the whole post, as More does for the card: it grows to the post's full width at 16:9 (or as wide as the post's height allows), centred and straightened, over 0.6 seconds, while the post's card fades away, the other tiles blur and the scene behind blurs as behind a focused contact field (`data-focus-blur`, `components/Background/focusBlur.ts`); pausing or ending it, a press anywhere outside it, or paging away pauses it and brings them back (`components/PostCollage.tsx`, `components/Media.tsx`, the place in `components/videoFill.ts`, tested by `videoFill.test.ts`); hovering a tile lifts it above the others (`components/PostCollage.tsx`, the motion in `.collage-tile` in `styles/globals.css`; with reduced motion they only fade). One notch of the wheel, one trackpad swipe (its coast included, however unevenly it rises and slows) or one press of an arrow, Page Up or Down or space glides straight to the next or previous post in 0.3 seconds, and so does a finger's swipe on a phone (40 pixels up or down; the browser's own scroll is held off meanwhile). A turn back the other way goes at once: if the next card has not set off yet the last one comes straight back, and if it has, it slides back out the way it came while the last one returns. A push the same way while the new card is still coming in (1.4 seconds) is let go, not kept for later, so one scroll, however long or uneven, or a burst of scrolling, turns one page and cards never pile up over each other; the page's first card arriving counts as a turn (`components/PageScroller/SnapPager.tsx`, logic in `paging.ts`, `turnGate`, `HOLD_MS` and `QUICK_MS`, tested by `paging.test.ts`). A post that More has opened taller than the page is scrolled through before the page turns: the wheel moves through it and stops at its end (a trackpad's coast stops there too), the arrow keys and the scroll arrows move most of a window at a time, and a finger drags through it; only a fresh push at its end turns the page, and paging back up onto it lands on its end (`throughPost` and `turnTo` in `paging.ts`; the browser's snapping is off while the page is on it). On a short screen (800 pixels tall or less) the text is set smaller so the longest post still fits. On a phone the fades are shorter, each post still fills one screen with its image tiles above its card, its text in one column, and a post with images shows four lines of its description (two on a screen 700 pixels tall or less) and hides the rest, with its technology, behind More; its badge sits in the title's row at its right, or on its own line at the left when the title leaves no room, and More and its links share a row, More at its left and the links at its right under the end of the text, so on a short phone its images keep about half the post.

## Gotchas

- A post's media `key` is a path under `public/` or, for a video, a YouTube id, and `main: true` makes an image its post's largest tile when the post has no video (`types/index.ts`); a misspelled path shows a broken image with no error. An image's `width` and `height` pick its crop; left out, it is cropped square.
- `Button` is shared with [contact](contact.md).
