# Home and the site frame

The home page and what surrounds every page: the header navigation, the footer with its contact line and social links, the 3D background, the 404 page, and the site's metadata, `robots.txt` and sitemap.

## Reach

`/`. The header links Home, Portfolio and Contact on a wide screen, and behind the menu button on a narrow one; Portfolio opens a glass dropdown of its two pages (see portfolio.md); every other page shows its title in the header. The 3D head is centred on a narrow screen (under 768px), except while a card covers the middle of it (the contact form, a post's card: `data-head-avoid="card"`), when it glides off the left edge and back as the card comes and goes; on a wider one it sits in the middle of the free space left of what the page shows beside it (the home page's title, the contact form, a post's card: anything marked `data-head-avoid`, measured where it rests, so a card sliding in does not pull the head along), gliding there when the page or window changes (a critically damped spring: it sets off gently and slows into place without overshooting, in about 0.9 seconds), and, where that space is narrower than its face, back to the middle of the screen, or off the left edge if what is beside it is a card, so it never shows behind a card's text (`components/Background/headPlace.ts`, tested by `headPlace.test.ts`; the camera moves in `HeadFraming.tsx`). Below 1024px the home page's name and subtitle sit at the top, above the head, the name on one line; on a phone (under 768px) the name is set to fill the window's width between the page's margins, the subtitle scaled with it, and the two are centred between the header's bottom and the top of the head, a fifth of the way down the window (`components/Home/Title.tsx`, `TitleBox.tsx`). As the page opens the name and subtitle slide in together from wholly off the window's right side, slowing gently to a stop over 1.4 seconds (`animate-from-right` in `tailwind.config.js`), and when a link to another page of the site is clicked they slide back off to the right, gathering speed (0.6 seconds), before the site goes there (`data-leave` in `components/Home/TitleBox.tsx`). Every page moves out this way before the site goes to another (`components/PageLeave.tsx`, mounted in `app/(root)/layout.tsx`): any card showing, the contact form or a post's card, slides on off the window's right side over 0.8 seconds, gathering speed, its glass whole while what is on it fades, a post's image tiles slide off to the right, the scroll arrows fade, and after 0.85 seconds the next page arrives as it does on opening (a click with a modifier key, a link to another site or a new tab, the browser's own back and forward, and reduced motion go at once; which clicks count is `slideAwayTo` in `components/leaveTo.ts`, tested by `leaveTo.test.ts`). The 3D letters follow their words both ways, and the head frames where the title rests, not where it is mid-slide. On a touch screen (no hover) there is no pointer to follow, so the head looks around on its own from the start, picking a new direction every 2 to 6 seconds. Tapping its face turns it quickly to the camera, angry and surprised; a second tap does the same, a third makes it really angry, and a fourth starts over. Left alone for 2.5 seconds it smiles, then goes back to looking around (`components/Background/headTaps.ts`, tested by `headTaps.test.ts`). A tap on a link, button, card, the header or the footer is meant for the page and leaves the head alone. While the 3D scene loads (a second or more, longer on a phone, with the page stalling as it does), what a page moves in as it loads waits where it starts, and the header, footer and scroll arrows are hidden; once the scene is drawing smoothly (three quick frames in a row, or after 6 seconds without it), the header, footer and arrows fade in over 0.8 seconds and the page's motion begins: the home title sliding in, the contact card, a post's card and its image tiles (`data-scene-ready` on `<html>`, set by `components/Background/sceneReady.ts`, tested by `sceneReady.test.ts`; the waiting in `styles/globals.css`). The header has no background or line of its own: it sits on the sky. Only the current page's link is underlined, like a link in running text (the accent, with the underline colour beneath it, turning the accent on hover); hovering or focusing another link turns it the accent with no underline (`components/Header/NavLink.tsx`). In the open phone menu every link is the card's ink and the current page keeps its underline; the menu's glass reaches past the screen's edges so its corners stay square. Page content fades out on an eased ramp as it scrolls up under it, the way the bust fades into the haze. The footer always shows at the bottom of the window, and content fades the same way as it scrolls down under it; the footer keeps `--footer-height` up to date so that fade starts at its top edge on any screen. The page scrolls inside a fixed full-window box (`components/PageScroller/index.tsx`) whose mask stays put, so no script runs while scrolling; the box's own scrollbar is left out of the mask so it shows whole, a strip as wide as it is (`--scrollbar-width`, kept by `components/PageScroller/ScrollbarWidth.tsx`; none where the scrollbar floats over the page), and the page inside is clipped to its own box, which ends where the scrollbar begins, so the cards' glass and the photo glass, which run past the window's right side, never cover it; the portfolio's pager and scroll arrows move that box. The fades are shorter on a phone than on a wide screen; both, and the CSS variables the page reads from them (`--page-top`, `--page-bottom`, `--post-height`, `--post-gap`), are in `components/PageScroller/edgeFade.ts`, tested by `edgeFade.test.ts`. On a wide screen, once nothing has moved on the home page for 10 seconds (no pointer, wheel, key, touch or scroll), the portfolio's scroll arrow, its chevrons sliding up on a close pane of the cards' glass, fades in just under the header's Portfolio, pointing up at it, and fades out again at the next move (`components/Home/NavHint.tsx`, the wait in `idleWatch.ts`, tested by `idleWatch.test.ts`); on a phone Portfolio is behind the menu button, so there is none. The footer's Contact me link opens `/contact`, and never breaks across lines. Any unknown path shows the 404 page in the site's frame (`app/(root)/[...missing]/page.tsx` calls `notFound()`, so `app/(root)/not-found.tsx` shows inside the site's layout, its card in `components/NotFound/NotFoundVideo.tsx`): its video on a frosted card as wide as the contact form's and placed the same, sliding in from the window's left edge as what is on it fades in, under "404 - Page not found". The video's window is 16:9, no taller than the space between the fades leaves it, and its picture fills it, cropped top and bottom, so the file's black sides never show; a play button sits over its bottom left, and clicking it or the video plays or pauses it. While it plays the scene behind the card blurs, as it does while a contact field has focus (`data-focus-blur` and `data-playing` on the video, read by `components/Background/focusBlur.ts`). While the page shows, the 3D head looks worried, with its own worried expression (the inner ends of its brows drawn up and together, its lips pressed and its mouth's corners turned down; `worried` in `scripts/head/expressions.mjs`, built into `head.bin` by `scripts/build-head.mjs`, tested by `headExpressions.test.ts`) in place of its usual reactions to the pointer, still following the pointer and wandering as everywhere else (`components/Background/headMood.ts`, read by `MyHead.tsx`); where the card covers the head (a phone), the head moves off the window as it does for the contact form.

## Verify

Roles: none; every page is public. No seed data: the pages render what the repository holds.

1. Open `/` at 1280 pixels wide: "Zack Greenbauer's" and "Portfolio and Sandbox" sit over the 3D background, the header shows Home, Portfolio and Contact, and the footer has the Contact me link and the GitHub, X and LinkedIn links.
2. Open `/` at 375 pixels wide: the head is centred, with "Zack Greenbauer's" on one line and the subtitle above it; at 1440 by 900 the head sits midway between the window's left edge and the title, and on `/contact` midway between the left edge and the form. Then at 375: the menu button opens Home, Portfolio and Contact, and choosing Portfolio opens `/portfolio` with Portfolio in the header.
3. On a phone (or a touch-emulating browser at 375 by 812), open `/`: the head looks around by itself; tap its face three times, a second apart: it faces the camera open-mouthed with narrowed eyes, again, then scowls with its lips pushed out; about 2.5 seconds after the last tap it smiles, and then looks around again.
4. Open `/no-such-page` at 1440 by 900: the header and footer show, "404 - Page not found" sits over the video on a card as wide as the contact form's, with a play button over the video's bottom left and no black at the video's sides, and the 3D head beside it follows the pointer with its brows lifted; clicking the play button plays the video and blurs the scene behind the card, and clicking it again pauses it.
5. Open `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt`: the sitemap lists the home page, the four portfolio pages and Contact; robots allows everything and names the sitemap.

No test that runs covers the pages themselves: `cypress/e2e/navigation.cy.ts` walks them, but it selects `.navbar`, `.section` and `.card`, which no component renders, and no workflow runs Cypress. The background head's geometry is covered by `components/Background/head.test.ts` and its expressions by `headExpressions.test.ts`, the sky's turn and forecast by `Sky.test.ts`, and how the name chooses its facing by `titleAim.test.ts`, all run by `npm test` on every pull request. What the name actually shows is checked by eye: the shader probe and the frame readback need a browser.

## Theme

The site's colours come from the head as it renders: `head-light` (its brightest point, the whitest of its eyes and reflections), then `head-peach`, `head-rose`, `head-purple`, `head-blue`, `head-indigo` and `head-navy` as its light falls off, to `head-dark` (its darkest skin). They are Tailwind colours in `tailwind.config.js` (`bg-head-rose`, `text-head-dark`) and CSS variables in `styles/globals.css` (`var(--head-rose)`). Light and dark are each the text colour on the other. Buttons use the whole range (`.bg-gradient-button`), resting on its light end and sweeping to its dark end on hover; on a light card the other way round, so they stand out from the glass (`components/Button.tsx`). Every card (each `section`, as on the contact page and the portfolio posts, and the 404 box) is frosted glass with big rounded corners: the 3D scene behind it drawn blurred, never tinted: it takes no colour the sky behind it does not have. The glass is just its frost and lenses: on a dark sky its text is light, on a light sky dark (the card's `data-sky`, and the `--card-*` colours in `styles/globals.css`, as the text over the sky switches), all of it at full strength, and the glass never takes a colour the sky behind it does not have: the tint pass runs only while the phone menu is open (`components/Background/Grading.tsx`), so even where the sky is near the middle the card's text can read below 4.5:1 for a moment until the sky moves on (a design choice: no tinted panes). A card switches sides only once the sky is clearly past the crossover, its text and glass crossfading together over 0.8 seconds, both fading out across the card's edge so the glass has no hard edge; the card itself has no background. The glass is pressed into small square lenses, like lenslet glass, sized so whole ones fit each card: each shows the lightly frosted scene behind it upside down and shrunk, as a lens does, so what is behind a card breaks into many small images and plain sky shows through unchanged. Photos lying under a card, or within 64px of it (a post's image tiles), get the same glass, easing in from nothing 64px out to whole just inside the card's edge, its lenses bending less and its frost fading rather than the glass turning see-through; the bending fades out far sooner than the frost (the strength cubed, `bendAt`), so lenses just past a photo do not show its edge flipped over the sky as a jagged, shadow-like band. The photo tiles themselves have no drop shadow, which the lenses also broke into stripes: the scene's glass cannot see the page's images, so over them it is a CSS `backdrop-filter` on a layer just behind the card (`data-photo-lens`, rendered by `components/EdgeCard.tsx` and placed every frame), outside the card so it fades and slides in with the card instead of popping on when the card's fade ends (a backdrop-filter inside an element fading by opacity sees nothing until it is opaque), an SVG filter of the same frost and lenses, the lenses lined up with the scene's, and, like the card's glass, changing no colours (`components/Background/photoLens.ts`; on only while a photo is within reach). Behind a card's form fields and paragraphs the glass is calmer, its lenses bending less over a slightly harder frost, fading in and out over 12px around each so it shows as no box, so what is behind does not distract from what is typed or read (`findFields` in `components/Background/glassCards.ts`). On a phone, or a screen under 600px tall, the glass is calmer: the whole card as calm as a field. It is not tinted either; instead, on a phone the head glides off the screen while a card covers it (see the head's placement above), so nothing busy shows behind the card's text. The footer sits on the sky on every screen, its text and icons following the sky like the header's (`components/Footer`); it was on the glass on phones only to match the cards' tint, and with no tint it only showed as a tinted pill while the phone menu's tint pass ran. While the phone menu is open its glass panel covers the page, and the 3D header title is not drawn over it (`menuOpen` in `focusBlur.ts`). Both are drawn in the scene (`components/Background/glass.ts`, passes in `Grading.tsx`) because the page scrolls in a masked box, and a mask stops CSS `backdrop-filter` inside it from seeing the canvas; they follow each card every frame and fade with the page under the header and footer (`fadeAt` in `components/PageScroller/edgeFade.ts`). The 404 page has no 3D scene, so its box is a tint with `backdrop-filter` over the video, its edges feathered by a CSS mask. The site has no greys: text off the cards takes `head-light` at reduced opacity, and on a card its text colour at full strength, form fields are a deeper see-through tint rounded like the buttons, and a disabled button is the resting button made see-through (`styles/globals.css`, `components/Button.tsx`). A link in a card's text (like Shapeways) is in the card's text colour, told apart by its underline (`--card-underline`: peach on a dark card, purple on a light one), which takes the brighter accent on hover (`--card-accent`); the forms' messages are in the text colour too, their words telling an error from success. Over the sky, a link in text takes the sky's accent, as the footer's Contact me does.

The page title in the header's top right (every page but home) is drawn like the home page's name: 3D letters in the head's skin reflecting the sky, turned to stand out from what is behind them (`components/Header/HeaderTitle.tsx` registers its words with `titleWords` after the name's; the name and a header title are never on one page). Text and icons over the 3D sky (the header's links, the footer's text and icons, and the portfolio pages' scroll arrows) follow the sky behind them like the home page's subtitle: `components/SkyText/useSkyColours.ts` has BackdropSampler read the picture behind each group a few times a second and sets `--sky-text`, `--sky-muted`, `--sky-faint`, `--sky-accent` and `--sky-underline` on it (`skyColours.ts`, tested by `skyColours.test.ts`). Text is `head-light` wherever the sky allows and darkens toward `head-dark` only as far as 4.5:1 needs; grey items and lines are the same colour, partly see-through (70% and 30%). What used to turn yellow or pink on hover, the Contact me link take the accent instead: peach over a dark sky, deep indigo over a light one, held to 3:1; the link's underline is rose or dusky purple. The head's few brightest reflections are left out of the reading, and each scroll arrow group reads what is behind it. Text only switches between light and dark once the sky is clearly past the point where both read equally (a margin either side), so a sky hovering there doesn't flicker it, and each change fades over a second and a half (the colours are registered with `@property`, so hover changes stay instant). The open phone menu is a pane of the cards' frosted glass over the whole screen (`data-glass`), its links in the card's ink, which follows the glass; the page behind it hides while it is open (`.mobile-menu` in `styles/globals.css`). The palette's single source is `theme/head.json`, which `tailwind.config.js` and the code both read.

## Gotchas

- The header's titles come from the `routes` list in `components/Header/index.tsx`, and the sitemap from the list in `app/sitemap.ts`. Both are kept by hand: a new page needs an entry in each.
- The site URL `https://www.zack.land` is written in `app/layout.tsx`, `app/robots.ts` and `app/sitemap.ts`.
- On a phone or small window (`isSmall` in `components/Background/smallScreen.ts`) the 3D scene is drawn at up to 1.5 device pixels per CSS pixel with no edge smoothing; elsewhere at up to 2, with 4 samples (`components/Background/quality.ts`). The smoothing is chosen once, as the scene loads, so a window resized across the breakpoint keeps it until a reload. Everything read back from the drawn frame (BackdropSampler, the cards' tints, the name's letters and its probe) goes through `components/Background/pixelReader.ts` and `usePixelReader.ts`, which never make the page wait on the GPU: results arrive a frame or two after the read. Read the frame through `usePixelReader`, never `readPixels` or `readRenderTargetPixels`: those stall the page until the GPU finishes, several milliseconds a time on a phone.
- The home page name is drawn in the 3D scene as flat letters in the head's own glossy skin, reflecting the same turning sky (`components/Background/TitleName.tsx`, font built by `node scripts/build-title-font.mjs` from Kanit, OFL, into `public/background/fonts/`). The real `<h1>` stays in the page, transparent, for screen readers, search and selection, and marks where each word sits for the 3D letters to follow (`components/Home/Title.tsx`). Both lines are lit as one curved sheet, so one sweep of the real sky's colours runs along the whole name. Which way the sheet faces is chosen a few times a second so every letter keeps at least 2:1 against the sky behind it (a deliberate floor below WCAG's 3:1 for large text: over the mid-toned sky, 3:1 leaves only near white and near black) while showing as much of the sky's palette as it can: a small offscreen grid renders, with three's own shader, what the letters would show at each of about 1,700 facings, now and where the sky is turning (`components/Background/titleProbe.ts`, `Sky.tsx`'s forecast); the frame's own pixels of the letters and the sky around them (`components/Background/titleMask.ts`) correct that prediction; and `components/Background/titleAim.ts` picks the facing. The name turns at the world's pace with a speed limit, keeps a target at least 1.5 seconds rather than hunting, and flips between lighter and darker than the sky only when its side cannot read. Fast pointer sweeps can still leave parts of it under 2:1 for a moment while it catches up. The subtitle stays white wherever the sky allows and darkens only as far as WCAG AA's 4.5:1 needs over a light sky, from the sky behind it as `components/Background/BackdropSampler.tsx` reads it back a few times a second.
- The background is three.js (`@react-three/fiber`); it renders on every page, so a change to it is a change to all of them.
- The background's look is pmndrs' "Color grading" sandbox (codesandbox.io/p/sandbox/wvgxp): a satin black head with eyes in `#aca8a3`, the blurred dawn HDR as the sky, and the `cubicle-99` LUT on top (`components/Background/Grading.tsx`). The head is `public/background/geometry/head.bin` (a compact binary format, gzipped because the site serves model files uncompressed; described and read by `components/Background/headGeometry.ts`): `myHead.obj`, smoothed by one subdivision, joined, as one welded surface, to a bust (neck, shoulders and chest) from MakeHuman's CC0 base mesh, shaped with MakeHuman's own muscle targets (pectorals, lats, deltoids, neck depth), its neck lengthened, its body leaned back under the head, and its shoulders and inner trapezius raised to match reference photos. `scripts/head/bridge.mjs` rebuilds the surface across the seam so it carries no crease, and `scripts/head/sculpt.mjs` adds the collarbones (with the top of the pectoral filled out below them), an Adam's apple and a fuller base to the back of the neck. The reference photos are front, side and back views; the outline matches all three except at the deltoids, where MakeHuman's arms are posed out from the sides. Rebuild it with `node scripts/build-head.mjs`. The head turns on its neck toward the pointer (and wanders when it is idle) while the shoulders follow only 15% of the turn: each vertex carries a head weight in `head.bin` (1 on the skull, falling to 0 down the neck, computed by `scripts/head/rig.mjs`), and `components/Background/headTurn.ts` turns it by that share in the vertex shader. The face reacts to where the head is looking (`components/Background/faceExpression.ts`): the brows rise a little when the pointer is above the eyes, and the eyes squint as if focusing when it is far from them, toward the other side of the screen; facing the camera, it smiles very faintly. When no one has done anything on the page for a few seconds (moved the pointer, scrolled by wheel, trackpad or keys, pressed, tapped or typed: `ACTIVITY` in `hooks/useMousePosition.ts`, tested by `useMousePosition.test.ts`), before the head starts wandering on its own it turns to face the camera while its face eases back to neutral, then, as it arrives, holds a surprised, open-mouthed look (the gasp) for 4 seconds, then blends from it into a smile held for 3 seconds; the greeting's expressions ease in and out about three times faster than the pointer reactions, so each change takes about a fifth of a second (`components/Background/idleGreeting.ts`). Each expression is built from what its muscles do in `scripts/head/expressions.mjs`; the smile (beyond that faint one), wide-eyed look and pout are built and matched to reference photos but nothing triggers them yet. The lips are parted along their seam onto a flat-black mouth with no teeth or tongue (`scripts/head/mouth.mjs`), and the gasp's dropped jaw is carried on down the throat so the neck stretches without a crease. The sky (and the head's reflections of it) turns with the head's same look target, mouse or idle wandering, as far as 90° either way: the pointer crossing the screen edge to edge turns it 180° (`components/Background/Sky.tsx`). Below the chin the bust sinks into a haze rising from below: it lightens toward lavender as it thickens and then lets the sky through, with an uneven top that drifts slowly upward, and its top is level over the neck and shoulders (`components/Background/neckFade.ts`). The sky and LUT files are served from `public/background/environment/`, not drei's CDN.
- Vercel Analytics loads on every page; the QAE gates declare its script as the one expected console and network error.
