## Reach

`/`. The header links Home, Portfolio and Contact on a wide screen, and behind the menu button on a narrow one; Portfolio opens a glass dropdown of its two pages (see portfolio.md); every other page shows its title in the header. The page's content keeps to a frame at most 1536px wide (the `container`, `data-frame` in `app/(root)/layout.tsx`), centred however wide the window, ultra-wide included, and the 3D head keeps to it too. On a narrow screen (under 768px) the head is centred, except while a card covers the middle of it (the contact form, a post's card: `data-head-avoid="card"`), when it glides off the left edge and back as the card comes and goes. On a wider one it sits in the middle of the free space between the frame's left edge and what the page shows beside it (the home page's title, the contact form, a post's card: anything marked `data-head-avoid`, measured where it rests, so a card sliding in does not pull the head along), or the frame's middle when there is nothing beside it; where a card beside it leaves too little room for its face (the post's card spanning the frame at tablet and small laptop widths, or the contact form), it glides off the left edge as on a phone, rather than sit behind the card's text. It glides each time the page or window changes (a critically damped spring: it sets off gently and slows into place without overshooting, in about 0.9 seconds; `components/Background/headPlace.ts`, tested by `headPlace.test.ts`; the camera moves in `HeadFraming.tsx`). While the phone menu is open the page's cards are hidden under its pane, so the head comes back behind the menu on every page and the pane's lens squares show over it as on the home page; it leaves again as the menu closes (`menuOpen` in `HeadFraming.tsx`). Below 1024px the home page's name and subtitle sit at the top, above the head, the name on one line; on a phone (under 768px) the name is set to fill the window's width between the page's margins, the subtitle scaled with it, and the two are centred between the header's bottom and the top of the head, a fifth of the way down the window (`components/Home/Title.tsx`, `TitleBox.tsx`). As the page opens the name and subtitle slide in together from wholly off the window's right side, slowing gently to a stop over 1.4 seconds (`animate-from-right` in `tailwind.config.js`), and when a link to another page of the site is clicked they slide back off to the right, gathering speed (0.6 seconds), before the site goes there (`data-leave` in `components/Home/TitleBox.tsx`). Every page moves out this way before the site goes to another (`components/PageLeave.tsx`, mounted in `app/(root)/layout.tsx`): any card showing, the contact form or a post's card, slides on off the window's right side over 0.8 seconds, gathering speed, its glass whole while what is on it fades evenly, a post's image tiles melt off to the right, the scroll arrows fade, and after 1.1 seconds the next page arrives as it does on opening (a click with a modifier key, a link to another site or a new tab, the browser's own back and forward, and reduced motion go at once; which clicks count is `slideAwayTo` in `components/leaveTo.ts`, tested by `leaveTo.test.ts`). The 3D letters follow their words both ways, and the head frames where the title rests, not where it is mid-slide. On a touch screen (no hover) there is no pointer to follow, so the head looks around on its own from the start. It looks around the way people glance about (`components/Background/wander.ts`, tested by `wander.test.ts`): it holds each look for 2.5 to 7 seconds, never quite still (a faint drift), then shifts to the next on a damped spring, setting off gently and settling without overshoot, bigger shifts taking longer (about one to two and a half seconds), so it looks around unhurried, about once every seven or eight seconds; smaller shifts come more often than big ones (typically about 20 degrees, now and then across its whole turning range), mostly side to side with some up and down, and about one in seven is a glance back toward the camera. Every look stays inside the head's turning range, so it never runs into its limits, and it moves the same at any frame rate. The sky trails behind its glances. Tapping its face turns it quickly to the camera, angry and surprised; a second tap does the same, a third makes it really angry, and a fourth starts over. Left alone for 2.5 seconds it smiles, then goes back to looking around (`components/Background/headTaps.ts`, tested by `headTaps.test.ts`). A click on the face with a mouse does the same, after which the head follows the pointer again and the idle clock starts over. Only the head counts (points of the skin whose head weight is over 0.9, `ON_HEAD` in `headTaps.ts`, tested by `headTaps.test.ts`), not the neck or shoulders, and a tap or click on a link, button, card, the header or the footer is meant for the page and leaves the head alone. On the page's first load a dark screen covers the whole page, the header and footer included, while the 3D scene loads (a second or more, longer on a phone), in the theme's darkest colour, as dark as the inside of the head's open mouth where the camera waits; its spinner is an 80px ring in the theme's light, peach and rose fading to nothing at its tail, on a faint track (`components/Background/Loader.tsx`). Once the scene is drawing smoothly (three quick frames in a row), the camera pulls straight back out of the mouth to its place over 3.4 seconds, eased in and out and slower still at the start, even in the log of its distance from the back of the mouth: it creeps out past the lips by about 1.1 seconds, then picks up; the spinner and the dark screen wait 0.75 seconds over the dark inside of the mouth and fade over the next second, as the face comes into view (`data-intro="fly"` on `<html>`; with no camera move they fade at once, the spinner in 0.3 seconds and the screen in 0.7), and the head's place on the page (the camera's view offset) comes in with it: the place it has with nothing covering it, beside what the page will show, until it has smiled (4.4 seconds), where it then stays unless a card will cover it there; so on a page whose card covers it (the contact form, a post on a phone; `intro.covered`, measured from where the page's cards will rest, shown yet or not, leaving out the cards of posts out of view, `data-off-view` in `components/EdgeCard.tsx`: on a tall window the first post's card can sit above the head while the next posts' cards would cover it, and the head went off and came back; `introPlaces` in `intro.ts`) it does the whole routine in view, then glides off the left edge, and the page moves in only once it is gone, 0.9 seconds later (`COVERED_PAGE_IN` in `intro.ts`, `open` and `resting` in `HeadFraming.tsx`), as a post's images loading as it moves in can stall a frame or two, which jumped the glide. The head is tipped up 0.5 radians while it does, so the open mouth (the dropped jaw has it face about 30 degrees down) faces the camera flat, levelling out from 2.7 to 3.4 seconds as the camera rests; the camera follows the mouth as it levels (`onFacingHead` in `intro.ts` works out where the head's shader puts it). While the mouth is open the inside of the head is lined in black (the skin's inner faces, `liningRef` in `MyHead.tsx`): looking into the tipped mouth the camera sees past its own dark inside, a single sheet, into the hollow head, and the sky showed through for a quarter second as a pale flash. The face it shows is surprised (the gasp's open mouth, brows raised high), which lets go from 2.3 to 3 seconds as the mouth closes and a smile comes in, whole as the camera rests at 3.4; the head, held facing the camera, smiling, until 4.4 seconds, then turns to the pointer (`components/Background/intro.ts`, tested by `intro.test.ts`; the camera moves in `HeadFraming.tsx`, the face and the look in `MyHead.tsx`). Clicks on the head do nothing until it has smiled. Whenever the head goes off the window on the view it was in (the intro on a covered page, the phone menu closing over a card), having been in the window there for over a second, the sky turns to a new station as for a change of view, its text with it (`headLeft` in `components/Background/skyViews.ts`, called from `HeadFraming.tsx`, tested by `skyViews.test.ts`); going off with a change of view (to the contact page on a phone) turns it once, for the new view, and so does any new view (a link, a page chosen in the phone menu, the head back behind it): the head's time on a view counts only from when it has taken that view's own place (the first load's, or one measured from the view's cards once they show), or from the menu opening over it, so its leaving as a new page's card arrives does not turn the sky again, however long the page took to come (`headSeen` and `turnsOnLeaving` in `skyViews.ts`). Until then what a page moves in as it loads waits where it starts, and the header, footer and scroll arrows are hidden; 2.35 seconds into the camera's move the header, footer and arrows fade in over 0.8 seconds and the page's motion begins: the home title sliding in, the contact card, a post's card and its image tiles, resting as the camera does (`data-page-in` on `<html>`, set by `intro.ts`; the waiting in `styles/globals.css`). With reduced motion, or where WebGL is drawn in software (no GPU, `drawsInSoftware` in `quality.ts`, a few frames a second), the camera does not move: the dark screen fades and the page moves in at once. If the scene has not drawn smoothly within 6 seconds (no WebGL, a very slow load, or WebKit drawing in software, seconds a frame), the dark screen fades and the page moves in without it (`READY_AT_MOST_MS` in `sceneReady.ts`), and the head appears in its place whenever the scene draws. The motion is CSS translate and opacity, which the browser runs off the main thread. Whether WebGL is drawn in software is asked from a worker (`askDrawsInSoftware` in `quality.ts`), as asking on the page's main thread stalls it (only where a worker cannot draw WebGL, Linux WebKit and older Safari, is it asked on the page), and the scene starts loading once that answer is in. Until the scene draws its first frame there (`data-software` and `data-scene-drawn` on `<html>`), the home page's name and a page's title in the header, which the scene draws in 3D, show as text instead and slide in with the page (`data-scene-title` in `styles/globals.css`). The header has no background or line of its own: it sits on the sky. Its edges keep to the frame's, as the footer's do: the first link's word (Home) starts at the frame's left edge, in line with the footer's icons, and the page title ends at its right edge (the nav in `components/Header/DesktopNav.tsx`, inside the header's own frame in `components/Header/index.tsx`). On a wide screen (768 pixels and up), where the browser shows a scrollbar that takes room (not one that floats over the page), the page's frame is centred in the narrower box beside it while the header and footer are centred in the whole window, so their edges and the frame's sit up to the scrollbar's width apart. On a phone the menu button and the title keep to the footer's edges: 32 pixels in from the window's sides, up to 704 pixels wide, and from there the 640-pixel frame centred in the window. Only the current page's link is underlined, like a link in running text (the accent, with the underline colour beneath it, turning the accent on hover); hovering or focusing another link turns it the accent with no underline (`components/Header/NavLink.tsx`). In the open phone menu every link is the card's ink and the current page keeps its underline; the menu's glass reaches past the screen's edges so its corners stay square. Page content fades out on an eased ramp as it scrolls up under it, the way the bust fades into the haze. The footer always shows at the bottom of the window (on the portfolio's pages, where the head can't fit beside the page, it holds only the scroll arrow; see portfolio.md), and content fades the same way as it scrolls down under it; the footer keeps `--footer-height` up to date so that fade starts at its top edge on any screen. The page scrolls inside a fixed full-window box (`components/PageScroller/index.tsx`) whose mask stays put, so no script runs while scrolling; the box's own scrollbar is left out of the mask so it shows whole, a strip as wide as it is (`--scrollbar-width`, kept by `components/PageScroller/ScrollbarWidth.tsx`; none where the scrollbar floats over the page), and the page inside is clipped to its own box, which ends where the scrollbar begins, so the cards' glass and the photo glass, which run past the window's right side, never cover it; the portfolio's pager and scroll arrows move that box. The fades are shorter on a phone than on a wide screen; both, and the CSS variables the page reads from them (`--page-top`, `--page-bottom`, `--post-height`, `--post-gap`), are in `components/PageScroller/edgeFade.ts`, tested by `edgeFade.test.ts`. On a wide screen, once nothing has moved on the home page for 10 seconds (no pointer, wheel, key, touch or scroll), the portfolio's scroll arrow, its chevrons sliding up on a close pane of the cards' glass, fades in just under the header's Portfolio, pointing up at it, and fades out again at the next move (`components/Home/NavHint.tsx`, the wait in `idleWatch.ts`, tested by `idleWatch.test.ts`); on a phone Portfolio is behind the menu button, so there is none. The footer's Contact me link opens `/contact`, and never breaks across lines. Any unknown path shows the 404 page in the site's frame (`app/(root)/[...missing]/page.tsx` calls `notFound()`, so `app/(root)/not-found.tsx` shows inside the site's layout, its card in `components/NotFound/NotFoundVideo.tsx`): its video on a frosted card as wide as the contact form's and placed the same, sliding in from the window's left edge as what is on it fades in, under "404 - Page not found". The video's window is always 16:9: as wide as the card, or narrower where the space between the fades (less the title) would otherwise cut it shorter, and its picture fills it, cropped top and bottom, so the file's black sides never show; a play button sits over its bottom left, and clicking it or the video plays or pauses it. While it plays the scene behind the card blurs, as it does while a contact field has focus (`data-focus-blur` and `data-playing` on the video, read by `components/Background/focusBlur.ts`). While the page shows, the 3D head looks worried, with its own worried expression (the inner ends of its brows drawn up and together, its lips pressed and its mouth's corners turned down; `worried` in `scripts/head/expressions.mjs`, built into `head.bin` by `scripts/build-head.mjs`, tested by `headExpressions.test.ts`) in place of its usual reactions to the pointer, still following the pointer and wandering as everywhere else (`components/Background/headMood.ts`, read by `MyHead.tsx`); where the card covers the head (a phone), the head moves off the window as it does for the contact form.

## Theme

The site's colours come from the head as it renders: `head-light` (its brightest point, the whitest of its eyes and reflections), then `head-peach`, `head-rose`, `head-purple`, `head-blue`, `head-indigo` and `head-navy` as its light falls off, to `head-dark` (its darkest skin). They are Tailwind colours in `tailwind.config.js` (`bg-head-rose`, `text-head-dark`) and CSS variables in `styles/globals.css` (`var(--head-rose)`). Light and dark are each the text colour on the other. Buttons use the whole range (`.bg-gradient-button`), resting on its light end and sweeping to its dark end on hover; on a light card the other way round, so they stand out from the glass (`components/Button.tsx`). Every card (each `section`, as on the contact page and the portfolio posts, and the 404 box) is frosted glass with big rounded corners: the 3D scene behind it drawn blurred, never tinted: it takes no colour the sky behind it does not have. The glass is just its frost and lenses: on a dark sky its text is light, on a light sky dark (the card's `data-sky`, and the `--card-*` colours in `styles/globals.css`, as the text over the sky switches), all of it at full strength, and the glass never takes a colour the sky behind it does not have: the tint pass runs only while the phone menu is on screen, open or fading away (`components/Background/Grading.tsx`), so even where the sky is near the middle the card's text can read below 4.5:1 for a moment until the sky moves on (a design choice: no tinted panes). A card takes its view's side as the sky sets off for it (in the moment before the scene has drawn the sky, only once the sky is clearly past the crossover), its text and glass crossfading together over 0.8 seconds, both fading out across the card's edge so the glass has no hard edge; the card itself has no background. The glass is pressed into small square lenses, like lenslet glass, sized so whole ones fit each card: each shows the lightly frosted scene behind it upside down and shrunk, as a lens does, so what is behind a card breaks into many small images and plain sky shows through unchanged. Photos lying under a card, or within 64px of it (a post's image tiles), get the same glass, easing in from nothing 64px out to whole just inside the card's edge, its lenses bending less and its frost fading rather than the glass turning see-through; the bending fades out far sooner than the frost (the strength cubed, `bendAt`), so lenses just past a photo do not show its edge flipped over the sky as a jagged, shadow-like band. The photo tiles themselves have no drop shadow, which the lenses also broke into stripes: the scene's glass cannot see the page's images, so over them it is a CSS `backdrop-filter` on a layer just behind the card (`data-photo-lens`, rendered by `components/EdgeCard.tsx` and placed every frame), outside the card so it fades and slides in with the card instead of popping on when the card's fade ends (a backdrop-filter inside an element fading by opacity sees nothing until it is opaque), an SVG filter of the same frost and lenses, the lenses lined up with the scene's, and, like the card's glass, changing no colours (`components/Background/photoLens.ts`; on only while a photo is within reach). Behind a card's form fields and paragraphs the glass is calmer, its lenses bending less over a slightly harder frost, fading in and out over 12px around each so it shows as no box, so what is behind does not distract from what is typed or read (`findFields` in `components/Background/glassCards.ts`). On a phone, or a screen under 600px tall, the glass is calmer: the whole card as calm as a field. It is not tinted either; instead, on a phone the head glides off the screen while a card covers it (see the head's placement above), so nothing busy shows behind the card's text. The footer sits on the sky on every screen, its text and icons following the sky like the header's (`components/Footer`); it was on the glass on phones only to match the cards' tint, and with no tint it only showed as a tinted pill while the phone menu's tint pass ran. While the phone menu is open its glass panel covers the page, and the 3D header title is not drawn over it (`menuOpen` in `focusBlur.ts`). Both are drawn in the scene (`components/Background/glass.ts`, passes in `Grading.tsx`) because the page scrolls in a masked box, and a mask stops CSS `backdrop-filter` inside it from seeing the canvas; they follow each card every frame and fade with the page under the header and footer (`fadeAt` in `components/PageScroller/edgeFade.ts`). The 404 page has the 3D scene too (its head looking worried), so its card is the same glass as every other. The site has no greys: text off the cards takes a theme colour at full strength (see the sky text below), and on a card its text colour at full strength, form fields are a deeper see-through tint rounded like the buttons, and a disabled button is the resting button made see-through (`styles/globals.css`, `components/Button.tsx`). A link in a card's text (like Shapeways) is in the card's text colour, told apart by its underline (`--card-underline`: peach on a dark card, purple on a light one), which takes the brighter accent on hover (`--card-accent`: the theme's light on a dark card, indigo on a light one); the forms' messages are in the text colour too, their words telling an error from success. Over the sky, a link in text takes the sky's accent, as the footer's Contact me does.

On a phone the header and the footer keep the same 2rem margin from the window's sides as a card's text and the home page's name, which fills the width between them (the frame's 0.75rem and a card's 1.25rem padding; `max-md:px-8` in `components/Header/index.tsx`, `max-md:mx-8` in `app/(root)/layout.tsx`).

The page title in the header's top right (every page but home; 24 pixels on a phone, a step bigger than elsewhere) is drawn like the home page's name: 3D letters in the head's skin reflecting the sky, turned to stand out from what is behind them (`components/Header/HeaderTitle.tsx` registers its words with `titleWords` after the name's; the name and a header title are never on one page). Text and icons over the 3D sky (the header's links, the footer's text and icons, and the portfolio pages' scroll arrows) follow the sky behind them like the home page's subtitle: `components/SkyText/useSkyColours.ts` has BackdropSampler read the picture behind each group a few times a second and sets `--sky-text`, `--sky-faint`, `--sky-accent` and `--sky-underline` on it (`skyColours.ts`, tested by `skyColours.test.ts`). When the view's side changes (below) the text turns over at once, on the `sky-side` event `Sky.tsx` sends, not at its next reading: those come every six frames, and where frames are slow one could fall after the phone menu's whole fade. Text is always one of the theme's colours, never a blend: `head-light` over a dark sky, and over a light one the lightest of `head-purple`, `head-blue`, `head-navy` and `head-dark` that reaches 4.5:1 (`darkText`); the header's other links, the social icons and the reCAPTCHA note take that same colour at full strength, never see-through; only the faint line between the phone menu's links is (30%). The footer's text can cross both the pale sky and the head's dark chest (a phone's home page), so each of its words takes its own colour from what is right behind it, light over the head and dark over the sky, without the resting sky's side: only the colour changes, with no backing, glow or shadow (`components/SkyText/SkyWords.tsx`, `ownSide` in `useSkyColours.ts`). What used to turn yellow or pink on hover, the Contact me link take the accent instead: peach over a dark sky (`head-light` where peach falls short of 3:1), deep indigo over a light one, never a blend; the link's underline is rose or dusky purple. The head's few brightest reflections are left out of the reading, and each scroll arrow group reads what is behind it. In the moment before the scene has drawn the sky, text only switches between light and dark once the sky is clearly past the point where both read equally (a margin either side), so a sky hovering there doesn't flicker it; after that it takes its view's side (below). Each change fades over 0.8 seconds, with the cards (the colours are registered with `@property`, so hover changes stay instant). The open phone menu is a pane of the cards' frosted glass over the whole screen (`data-glass`), its links in the card's ink, which follows the glass; the page behind it, and the footer, hide while it is open (`.mobile-menu` in `styles/globals.css`). The palette's single source is `theme/head.json`, which `tailwind.config.js` and the code both read.

## Gotchas

- The mouth's dark inside (`mouth` in head.bin) is one sheet shaped by the jaw, not a closed cavity, and the skin draws only its outer faces, so from most angles into an open mouth the sky shows through the head. The first load's intro lines the head in black while it holds the mouth open (`MyHead.tsx`); anything else that looks into the mouth needs the same.
